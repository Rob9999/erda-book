I think the strongest model would be a 4+1 structure for Europe.

Not a simple “associated membership” as a waiting room, but a legally structured path of democratic and domain-based integration.

1. A global ring of EU friends and democratic partners, such as Canada, Japan, Australia, South Korea and others.
2. A first democratic accession ring, entered by a democratic decision of the applicant country.
3. A domain-based membership ring, where countries gain rights and duties step by step by fulfilling specific EU domains: single market, customs, energy, digital policy, research, security cooperation, justice standards, etc.
4. Full EU membership, where all EU rules and all EU domains are fulfilled and enforceable before EU courts.
5. A deeper constitutional core for countries that want to go further: common foreign policy, common defence, stronger fiscal coordination, Euro/EZB integration and possibly a shared European ministry structure.

This would solve the current all-or-nothing problem. Countries like Ukraine, Moldova or the Western Balkans would not be trapped in a vague waiting room, but could move forward wherever they are ready. At the same time, full EU membership would remain serious, rights-based and legally enforceable.

And for the inner core, I would not call it only “European Federation” at first, because that may scare people. A better public name could be ERDA: a European Rights-Based Democratic Union — a deeper democratic and constitutional core inside Europe, open to all who are ready.
